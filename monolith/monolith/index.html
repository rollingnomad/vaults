    PRINCIPLES

    CLASSLESS

    A character's role or skills are not limited by a single class. Instead, the equipment they carry and their experiences defines their specialty. They have a background, which tells you a bit about their experiences before becoming an adventurer, but it doesn't define who they can become.

    MODULARITY

    All rules are optional. Everything is designed to be modular. You can pull whole parts out, slot in optional rules, and add your own home brew content.

    NARRATIVE GROWTH

    Characters are mostly changed through in-world actions and consequences. Players may acquire new skills and abilities by surviving dangerous events or overcoming obstacles. Dangerous and valuable technology and artifacts will often change a character. Characters also have the incentive of acquiring experience points via credits recovered from hunting rare objects or completing dangerous jobs.

    MINIMAL IMPLIED SETTING

    Some setting elements are implied, such as alien species, psionics and space magic, starships, cybernetics, etc. Most of the setting was kept intentionally vague so you can customize your galaxy and it contents. If you don't want aliens, psionics, or anything else, they can be re-flavored or removed.

    HACKABLE

    Many of the effects of psionics, astromancy, talents, experimental tech, artifacts, and various augmentations can be shifted around. Magic effects can be items instead. A piece of tech could be an ability or talent instead. There's a lot of overlap, and that's intentional. Hack and re-flavor as needed.

    COMPATIBILITY

    This game is designed to be compatible with other pre-written adventures (especially sci-fi) with minimal and flexible conversion.

    SPRAWLING ADVENTURE

    While single sessions are a perfectly acceptable ways to play, this game was designed with several sessions to campaign length play in mind. Characters may start out with a few more advantages than in other games, but that's by design. The universe is still deadly enough to impose serious challenges when needed.

    DANGER & CONSEQUENCES

    Characters may be powerful, but they are also vulnerable to danger. Death is always around the corner, but it is never random or without warning. Sometimes there are consequences other than death that can change who you are, or even alter the galaxy itself.

    FICTION FIRST

    Dice do not always reflect an obstacle's difficulty or its outcome. Instead, success and failure are arbitrated by the GM in dialogue with the players, based on in-world elements. Always rule in favor of fun if everyone is in agreement.

    PLAYER CHOICE

    Players should always understand the reasons behind the choices they've made, and information about potential risks. Give players a lot of information.

    SHARED OBJECTIVES

    Players should trust one another to engage with the shared setting, character goals, and party challenges. Therefore, the party is typically working together towards a common goal, as a team.

    GROWTH & PROGRESSION

    EXPERIENCE GROWTH

    Characters will gain XP and level up as a measure of general toughness. They gain heartiness and become more capable in a general, non-specific sense.

    NARRATIVE GROWTH

    This is the kind of progression and change that happens from in-world events via gameplay. Narrative growth comes from finding rare artifacts, evolving psionic abilities, augmenting yourself with the latest black market cyberware, NPC connections, and even suffering losses. All of these contribute to a character changing narratively.

    APPLYING GROWTH

    This is where the role of the GM really comes into play. Some backgrounds provide implied fiction to aid both the player as they navigate who their character might be, and guide the GM as they adjudicate narrative growth based on player action.

    If a PC rolls 'Psionic' as their background, they can lean into that element of their character or completely disregard it. Find opportunities for the them to engage in the aspects of their character they find most interesting. For example, reward the Psionic with things like awakening new powers from stressful events, learning from fellow psionics, and seeding growth opportunities in your setting for them to discover- like items, opportunities to train, and other ways to help them grow and change based on their choices in the fictional world.

    If players forego their background in lieu of wanting to become someone new, support that in the same way.

    Let the universe change the characters.

    CHARACTER CREATION

    ABILITY SCORES

    Roll 3D6 for each ability score in order. The player may swap any two of the results.

    • › Strength (STR) Power, toughness, grit, & stamina.
    • › Dexterity (DEX) Quickness, precision, agility, & technological aptitude.
    • › Willpower (WIL) Confidence, force of personality, mental fortitude, & affinity for the weird.

    BACKGROUND

    • › Roll 1D12 and consult the corresponding background to see what your life has been like up to this point.
    • › Next, roll on all the tables listed under that background.

    HIT PROTECTION (HP)

    Rolling 1D6 determines your character's starting Hit Protection (HP), which reflects their ability to avoid damage in combat. HP does not indicate a character's health or fortitude; nor do they lose it for very long (see Healing). They grow more grizzled over their experiences. If an attack takes a PC's HP exactly to 0, the player must roll on the scars table.

    FINISHING TOUCHES

    Roll on any the additional tables (if you want) in the finishing touches section (pg. 32) for extra traits and character qualities.

    SHARED DEBT

    Roll on the shared debt table (pg. 34) for the group's shared debt. This will help facilitate urgency for the players by prompting them to search out jobs and make money, as well as provide the GM with world building and story hooks. Optional but highly suggested.

    VICES

    With your Ability Scores in place, make a WIL save. If you fail, roll on the vices table to start with a vice. Consequently, characters may develop vices through the fiction of play. See the triggers listed in vices to learn more about this. Optional but highly suggested.

    VICES

    ABOUT VICES

    Vices serve as both character growth and a way to pressure PC's pockets and keep them continuing to adventure & acquire wealth to support their vice. A vice is a nice way to bleed a player's wealth as they begin to accumulate more. A player typically shouldn't have more than 1-2 vices.

    GAINING A VICE

    Make a WIL Save after character creation. Upon failure, roll a D10 and begin with that vice.

    Vices may also be acquired through the fiction of play. Consult the triggers for suggestions on what might trigger a character to develop a vice. A character must pass a WIL save after hitting a trigger or acquire that vice.

    SATISFYING VICES

    Vices must be satisfied once a week or the PC must pass a WIL save. Failing a WIL Save adds the deprived condition until they satisfy their vice. Satisfying a vice should cost money in some way, whether directly with money or indirectly via resources.

    GAMBLING

    Playing cards, throwing dice, or other forms of risking money on games of chance.

    ALCOHOL

    Drinking ale, wine, spirits, or other alcoholic beverages that intoxicate your senses.

    DRUGS

    Narcotic delights to smoke, snort, or consume in search of oblivion.

    LUST

    Pleasures of the flesh such as dalliances with strangers or paid sex workers.

    LUXURY

    Expensive clothing, decadent food, art, and general opulence.

    WEIRD

    Consorting with ancient beings, contacting unknown entities, and performing forgotten or forbidden rituals.

    OBLIGATION

    Devotion to a cause, organization, charity, or family.

    VIOLENCE

    Spectating extreme violence or participating in the bloodshed yourself.

    FAITH

    Dedication to a power, deity, ancestry, or other belief system.

    AMBITION

    Pursuing a personal goal, vendetta, or longterm project.

    D10 Vice Triggers Satisfying

    2 Alcohol Consuming large amounts of booze, drinking heavily for multiple days. Binging heavily, buying rounds for the whole bar, brewing your own booze.

    CORE RULES

    ABILITY SCORES

    Each of the three abilities are used in different circumstances.

    • › Strength (STR) Used for saves requiring physical power, like lifting heavy things, bending or breaking, resisting poison, endurance, stamina, etc.
    • › Dexterity (DEX) Used for saves requiring speed, reflexes, agility, and poise, like dodging, sneaking, climbing, balancing, using complex tech, etc.
    • › Willpower (WIL) Used for saves to persuade, deceive, interrogate, intimidate, charm, provoke, manipulate alien technology, navigate star charts, harness psionics, etc.

    SAVES

    A save is a roll to avoid bad outcomes from risky choices and circumstances. PCs roll a d20 for an appropriate ability score. If they roll equal to or under that ability score, they pass. Otherwise, they fail. A 1 is always a success, and a 20 is always a failure. Many risks that call for a save bypass HP altogether. Communicate the risk clearly to your players if they have a chance to decide on a different course of action. If there is a contested action, the party at most risk makes the save.

    Opposed Saves: Both parties roll and the highest successful save wins.

    LUCK

    Sometimes you just need to let the fates decide on an outcome. If you don't know about if something should be a save, or you don't quite know how to calculate if something is good or bad, roll a d6. On a 1 the result is unfavorable, 2-3 the result is mixed or uncertain, 4-5 is normal, 6 is favorable.

    ADVANTAGE & DISADVANTAGE

    When a character has some sort of leverage or is at some sort of handicap, and a save is required, give them enhanced success or reduced failure for ADV and mixed success or disastrous failure for DIS .

    INVENTORY

    Characters have a total of 10 inventory slots: a backpack with six slots, and four slots for their body (such as carried weapons, chest, legs, feet, head, etc.)

    Anyone carrying a full inventory (e.g. filling all 10 slots) is reduced to 0 HP.

    Most items take up one slot, and small items may sometimes be bundled together. Slots are abstract and can be rearranged per the GM's discretion.

    • › Bulky items take up two slots and are typically two-handed or awkward to carry.
    • › Heavy items and weapons can only be used or moved within one round.
    • › Unwieldy items cannot be carried by one person or in your inventory. Additional means of transportation must be conceived.

    A PC cannot carry more items than their inventory allows. Carriers (which must be controlled with both hands), vehicles, droids, or pack animals can increase inventory. Hired mercenaries can also be paid to carry equipment and also have 10 slots.

    TASK RESOLUTION

    When a player attempts to do something such as pick a lock, drive a speeder, or hack a computer, three task resolution factors should be taken into consideration: training, tools, and time.

    If a player has all three: training (inferred from background, a skill, etc.), appropriate tools (an item), and enough time (usually 10 mins), they will alway succeed.

    If a player is missing one of the above , a Save might be appropriate, with a failed save resulting in a consequence, such as getting caught, breaking the tool, or not being able to solve the problem.

    If a player is missing two of the above , it is usually impossible to resolve the task at hand, or the results of failing a Save could potentially be very dangerous.

    HEALING

    Resting for a few moments and having a drink of water restores lost HP but leaves the party exposed. Ability score loss (see Critical Damage) can usually be restored by recuperating for a few days facilitated by a medic, doctor, ship med-bay, or other appropriate source of expertise. Some of these services are cheap, while experts, unusual services, or more expedient means of recovery may come at a higher cost or favor.

    DEPRIVATION & FATIGUE

    A PC deprived of a crucial need (such as food, warmth, or rest) is unable to recover HP or ability scores. Anyone deprived for more than a day adds fatigue to their inventory, one for each day. You can also gain fatigue in other ways during play such as from ability use and specific events. Each fatigue occupies one slot and lasts until they are able to recuperate (such as a full night's rest in a safe spot).

    CONDITIONS

    Conditions occupy inventory just like fatigue, but have other effects and unique recovery conditions.

    ARMOR

    Before calculating damage to HP, subtract the target's armor value from the result of damage rolls. No one can have more than 3 points of armor.

    Worn armor is usually listed as Armor 1, Armor 2, etc. and will usually only benefit from a secondary armor source if it is bonus (+1 Armor) or it makes sense fictionally.

    Shields and similar items provide bonus defense (e.g. +1 armor), but only while the item is held or worn.

    SHIELD SHORTING

    Players can opt to absorb all of the damage from one attack in exchange for their shield generator being destroyed.

    COST OF LIVING

    PCs can rest on their ship (for free) or at paid lodgings, and will recover from most minor and moderate wounds after 7 days of rest. If players don't sleep in a dedicated sleeping location, they will be deprived until they are properly rested.

    WEALTH & TREASURE

    The most common currency is the galactic credit (cred or C). Regional currencies may exist and have an exchange rate. Some systems might only take a certain currency depending on their government and territory.

    Treasures are highly valuable items or goods, usually bulky, and rarely useful beyond their value. Treasure can be a lure, taking PCs to exotic and even dangerous locations, and is often under the protection of intimidating foes. Space stations, trade ports, and planetary cities barter and trade based on the local rarity and value of an item or commodity.

    FREELANCERS & MERCS

    PCs can hire freelancers and mercenaries to help on missions or with other work. To create a freelancer, roll 3D6 for each ability score, then give them 1D6 HP and a standard weapon (D6), then roll on the Finishing Touches tables to further flesh them out. Freelancers cost between 50-100 credits per day, or a share of whatever rewards the party obtains. After a freelancer completes a mission they can be taken on as a crew member and gain levels like the PCs. From then on they will always take an equal share of pay.

    TAGS

    Tags are optional, simple descriptors that state truths about a character. They don't offer direct mechanical bonuses, but can be used as a tool when negotiating between the player and the GM, or deciding an outcome.

    Backgrounds also imply a non-specified amount of traits, skills, knowledge, or training and can also be leveraged as such.

    Character qualities and skills may be listed explicitly as tags, such as [Technical Savvy] for an Engineer background. A PC could also spend a significant amount of time or money training (or via similar means) to learn various skills. Using tags can be a helpful way to help organize a character's acquired knowledge and expertise.

    As characters interact with the setting, they will acquire more capabilities, and more truths about their character will be defined, either as tags, or recorded as notes on the character sheet.

    EXPERIENCE POINTS

    Experience points (XP) is gained from credits recovered (returned to a 'safe' location). XP from treasure must be acquired by finding a buyer for the treasure. XP can also be gained from carousing (pg. 52-53)

    LEVELING UP

    Re-roll all three of your ability scores. If the new result is higher, increase that score by 1. Roll 1d6 and add to HP total. Each time you level up, reset XP to zero.

    Optional Adjustments

    • › Adjust HP gain to 1D4 for a slightly slower progression.
    • › Adjust HP gain to gain +1 for rolling over current HP with a 3D6 roll for a more consistent, but even slower progression.
    • › For slower leveling, simply adjust the experience needed to start with 1,000 and either add +1,000 each level, or double it every level.

    COMBAT RULES

    START OF COMBAT

    At the Start of Combat, each PC must make a DEX Save for a chance to act before their adversaries. If PCs fail their DEX Save, they don't act in the first turn. Subsequent rounds will always go PC Turn, then Enemy Turn.

    If one side is surprised or ambushed, they automatically go second at the start of combat.

    ROUNDS

    The game typically plays without strict time accounting. In a fight or circumstance where timing is helpful, use rounds to keep track of when something occurs. A round is roughly ten seconds of in-game time and consists of turns.

    TURNS

    • › Each round, PCs all act together on the PC turn, and enemies all act together on the enemy turn. The only exception to this is the start of combat, where passing a DEX Save allows PCs to act before the enemy turn.
    • › The GM should telegraph the most likely actions taken by NPCs or monsters.

    ACTIONS

    On the PC's turn, each character may move up to 40ft and take up to one action. This could be attacking, using a power or item, moving another 40ft, or some other reasonable action.

    Each round, the PCs declare what they are doing before dice are rolled. If a character attempts something risky, the GM calls for a save for appropriate players or NPCs.

    ATTACKING & DAMAGE

    The attacker rolls their weapon's damage dice and subtracts the target's armor, then deals the remaining total to their opponent's HP. Any damage that exceeds HP is dealt to STR (see Critical Damage.) When an attack reduces a PC's HP to exactly 0, they gain a scar (see Scars).

    ATTACK MODIFIERS

    • › If fighting from a position of weakness (such as through cover or with bound hands), the attack is impaired and the attacker must roll 1d4 damage regardless of the attack's damage die.
    • › If fighting from a position of advantage (such as against a helpless foe or through a daring maneuver), the attack is enhanced , allowing the attacker to roll 1d12 damage instead of their normal die.
    • › Certain advantageous situations or tools might call for enhanced damage, or bonus damage. Use at your own discretion as a reward for clever planning.

    BONUS DAMAGE

    Under certain circumstances, an attack may recieve a bonus, noted as +D6, +D8 etc. This is rolled as an extra attack against the same target, following the same rules as dual weapons. Remember that only the highest single die result causes damage. Attacks against a completely helpless target always receive +d12.

    MULTIPLE ATTACKERS

    If multiple attackers target the same foe, roll all damage dice and keep the single highest result each round.

    DUAL WEAPONS

    If attacking with two weapons at the same time, roll both damage dice and keep the single highest result each round.

    BLAST DAMAGE

    Attacks with the blast quality affect all targets in the noted area, rolling separately for each affected character. Blast refers to anything from explosions to huge cleaving onslaughts to the impact of a meteorite. If unsure how many targets can be affected, roll the related damage die for a result.

    CRITICAL DAMAGE

    Damage that reduces a target's HP below zero decreases a target's STR by the amount remaining. They must then make a STR save to avoid critical damage. Additionally, some enemies will have special abilities or effects that are triggered when their target fails a critical damage save. Rare or exceptional items and abilities may also give PCs special effects on crits.

    Any PC that suffers critical damage cannot do anything but crawl weakly, grasping for life. If given aid and rest, they will stabilize. If left untreated, they die within the hour.

    WEAPON RANGES

    Weapons have one or more ranges for their optimal use. Any time a weapon is used a single increment outside of its listed range, the damage is impaired. A weapon cannot be used more than one increment outside of its range. Range descriptions are rough estimates. If a weapon in the armory doesn't list a range, make a call based on what seems appropriate.

    • › Close (C-R): Within melee & touch range.
    • › Short (S-R): In the same room.
    • › Mid (M-R): Across a yard or down a street.
    • › Long (F-R): Across a field or city block.

    ABILITY SCORE LOSS

    If a PC's STR is reduced to 0, they die. If their DEX is reduced to 0, they are paralyzed. If their WIL is reduced to 0, they are delirious or catatonic. Complete DEX or WIL loss renders the character unable to act until they are restored through extended rest or by extraordinary means.

    CHARACTER DEATH

    When a character dies, the player is free to create a new character or take control of a mercenary. They immediately join the party in order to reduce downtime.

    DETACHMENTS

    Large groups of similar combatants fighting together are treated as a single detachment. When a detachment takes critical damage, it is routed or significantly weakened. When it reaches 0 STR, it is destroyed.

    • › Attacks against detachments by individuals are impaired (excluding blast damage).
    • › Attacks against individuals by detachments are enhanced and deal blast damage.

    MORALE CHECKS

    Enemies must pass a WIL save to avoid fleeing when they take their first casualty and again when they lose half their number. Some groups may use their leader's WIL in place of their own. Lone foes must save when they're reduced to 0 HP. Morale does not affect PCs.

    RETREAT

    Running away from a dire situation always requires a successful DEX save, as well as a safe destination to run to.

    ADVANCED WEAPONRY RULES

    RANGED PROPERTIES

    • › Cheap: Roll a D6 after any combat encounter, or as fiction might dictate. A roll of 1 and it breaks.
    • › Armor Piercing (AP) : Ignore all armor (damage rolls of 1 misfire)
    • › Exploding: Re-roll max damage dice and add to total.
    • › Spec Ammo: Special ammo, charge, or fuel. Max damage rolls require spending a turn reloading/refueling and consume a unit of spec ammo from your inventory.
    • › Overclocked: On max damage, upgrade dice type. 6>8>10. 10 on a d10 will reset the weapon to its base dice and the weapon will need 2d6 hours to recharge. Can manually re-set the battery outside of combat for 1d6 hours recharge.

    MELEE PROPERTIES

    • › Parry : Melee attacks of 2 damage or less are harmlessly parried away.
    • › Grapple: (no damage) Entangle your opponent. They will need to pass a STR Save to break free.
    • › Sweep: ( Bulky) Long weapons that allow a second attack on an adjacent opponent.
    • › Brutal: (Bulky) Big enough to instantly kill an opponent on max damage and force a morale check on the target's allies.
    • › Vorpal: (Small Blades) Damage immediately bypasses HP, going straight to STR and requiring a save against Critical Damage. Typically illegal.
    • › Deadly: (Small or Medium Blades) Deadly weapons are fast enough to roll again on max damage and add to total.

    DAMAGE TYPES

    • › Thermal: DEX Save or take 1D4 heat damage for 1D4 rounds.
    • › Cryo: STR Save or do half damage on next attack.
    • › Shock: DEX Save or lose turn for 1 round.
    • › EMP: (Only effective vs machines) STR save or lose turn for 1 round.
    • › Biological: (Only effective vs organics) STR save or 1D4 damage for 1D4 rounds.
    • › Serrating: (Only effective vs organics) STR Save or 1D4 damage for 1D4 rounds.
    • › Sonic: (Only effective vs organics) WIL Save or lose turn for 1 round.
    • › Disintegration: DEX Save vs instant obliteration. Usually illegal.

    SMART WEAPONS

    Smart weapons fire A.I. guided rounds. Roll the two dice indicated and take only the highest single result. Must have smart weapon ammo in your inventory to use smart weapons.

    • › When you roll doubles, no damage and the weapon glitches. You must spend the next round recalibrating.

    HIGH-ENERGY WEAPONS

    These weapons do higher damage at the risk of possible malfunction. Roll 2 damage dice instead of one (example, 2d6) and the total is combined. High-energy weapons cannot have other properties.

    • › When you roll doubles, no damage and the weapon overheats. You must spend the next round venting heat.
    • › When you roll max (on either dice) you must spend the next round reloading. This requires ammo in your inventory.
    • › High-energy ammo can be bundled in stacks of 3.

    TACTICAL PROCEDURES

    TURNS

    • › When exploring a dangerous location such as an ancient ruin, derelict starship, or other adventure site, GMs have the option of using turns.
    • › Each turn is roughly 10 minutes (10 combat rounds equal 1 exploration turn).
    • › If you're mapping on a grid, use 10-foot squares .

    TACTICAL MOVEMENT

    • › Characters can carefully explore about 120 feet (3-times their combat movement of 40) per turn. This accounts for moving at a quiet pace, mapping progress, and checking for traps, hazards, and secrets. They will spot most traps and clues at this pace.
    • › Characters can quickly explore about 240 feet per turn. Players moving quickly may get a chance to roll under WIL to notice dangers, otherwise they may trigger any hidden trap (2-in-6).
    • › Characters can move recklessly about 600 feet per turn. Characters running at a full sprint will see no signs of danger and their chance of triggering a hidden trap increases to 4-in-6.

    TRAPS & DANGERS

    • › Traps should be designed to be disarmed or subverted with ingenuity and creative thinking using common sense and items, not skills and rolls. If you design an oldschool trap, like a pressure plate with a 1 in 6 chance of discovering and 2 in 6 chance of triggering, make it more interesting than simply 'gotcha' damage.
    • › Consider what might make exploration more interesting. Could interacting with something open up a new area? Are there multiple factions at odds with each other the players can use strategically?

    SEQUENCE OF PLAY

    1. Encounter Roll: GM makes encounter roll.
    2. Actions: The party decides what action to take (e.g. moving, searching, listening, entering or exiting rooms).
    3. Description: GM describes what happens. If encounters were rolled, they happen here. If (1) was rolled on the encounter die, follow the subsequent procedures:
    4. › Roll 3d6 x 10 to determine how far the NPCs are, distance is in feet.
    5. › Roll for NPC Reactions and NPC Wants.
    6. › Have the PCs make DEX saves to determine start of combat, but don't engage in combat.
    7. › If NPCs go first , determine if they will attack, wait, or do something else based on their reaction and wants.
    8. › If PCs are first , determine if they will attack or wait. If PCs ask to gauge the NPCs disposition out of an interest to negotiate or talk, be honest with them if it's obvious.
    9. End of turn: Update time. Manage light, food, need to rest, or any item usage and tracking.

    ENCOUNTER ROLL

    Roll a d6 encounter dice every 3 turns. Deadlier dungeons can be rolled every 2 turns. Only a roll of 1 will result in an encounter, and they won't always be hostile.

    1. Encounter: Roll on an encounter table, then roll for NPC Reaction and NPC Wants.
    2. Sign: Clue, track, abandoned lair, scent, victim, droppings, etc. On the next Encounter Roll, consider encountering whatever left the sign if applicable.
    3. Locality: Context-dependent timer, water rising, ritual completing, environmental occurrence.
    4. Exhaustion: Rest next turn or deprived.
    5. Expiration: Resource expires, ongoing effects end, light usage devices glitch or fail, bellies rumble.
    6. Discovery or Treasure: Players find valuable information, location knowledge, hidden feature, or loot.

    SEARCHING

    • › A quick search takes 1 minute, covers roughly a 30x30' space, and reveals only the most obvious information.
    • › A detailed search takes 10 minutes (1 round), covers roughly a 30x30' space, and reveals all hidden information.
    • › If players state they're searching in the exact spot of a hidden feature, or are interacting specifically with a hidden mechanism, they will succeed.

    LIGHT

    • › Lanterns, glo-torches, and other radial sources of light illuminate 40' clearly and provide dim outlines and shadows for a further 40'.
    • › Flashlights have a range of about 80' clearly and another 40' dimly, but only a width of a few feet.
    • › In the dark, light sources are visible miles away.

    Most treasure should be more valuable than it is useful. It should generally hard to acquire, big, heavy, and useless beyond its value. Make it something someone other than the players wants. If they don't have a buyer or someone who hired them to find it, they should be encouraged to go out and see who might want to buy it. Players can also loot cred sticks, weapons, armor, items, and various powerful artifacts and technology. Maps to other locations and valuable information are also great sources of loot.

    HEXCRAWL TRAVEL

    HEX CRAWLING

    Each hex represents 6 miles. An adventuring day is divided into 6 turns of approximately 4 hours each. Two of these phases, approximately 8 hours, must be spent sleeping in order to avoid becoming deprived. In each turn, the following procedure is followed:

    1. Action: The party decides on one action for that turn.
    2. Event Roll: The GM rolls on the event dice for encounters and other random events.
    3. Resolve: The action and event rolls are resolved.

    EVENT ROLL

    The GM rolls 1d6 and consults the table appropriate to the party's location.

    1. Encounter: Roll on an encounter table for that terrain type or location.
    2. Sign: Clue, spoor, or indication of nearby encounter, locality, hidden feature, or information about a nearby hex.
    3. Locality: Shifts in weather, terrain, or other local changes.
    4. Expiration: Exhaustion (Rest this turn or add 1 fatigue), Hunger (Eat a ration or add 1 fatigue), or another resource is needed.
    5. Discovery: Discover something useful such as food, treasure, or other resources.
    6. Hidden Feature: The main feature of the hex is discovered, or, choose or roll randomly for a unique hex feature from a random table. Options could be small dungeons, secret areas, factions, special items, etc. If the hex doesn't have a hidden feature, consider an additional discovery and be clear that the hex has been fully explored.

    EXPLORATION ACTIONS

    The party may engage in one of the following standard actions each turn:

    • › Travel: Passing through a hex and into an adjacent one. On roads marked on the map, the party travels through two hexes (three if mounted). Off-road, one hex is traversed. There is also a 2-in-6 chance, when traveling off-road, of getting lost. This is increased to 3-in-6 in hexes classified as difficult terrain. An experienced survivalist decreases the chance of getting lost by 1-in-6. If lost, PCs will need to spend the next turn using the 'search' action to reorient themselves. The effects of getting lost are rolling a D6, assigning a number to each side of the hex, and moving the players one hex off course in that direction.
    • › Explore: Looking for interesting features within a hex. There is a 4-in-6 chance of discovering the hidden location in the hex description. Difficult terrain reduces this to 3-in-6. An experienced explorer increases the chance by 1-in-6.
    • › Search: Looking for something which was previously encountered in a hex. The basic chance is 5-in-6 or 4-in-6 in difficult terrain. An experienced woodsman, tracker, or navigator increases the chance by 1-in-6.
    • › Interact: Staying in the current location (e.g. exploring a dungeon, town, etc).
    • › Camp: Resting and/or sleeping.
    • › Forage: Hunt, fish, or forage for food. There is a basic 3-in-6 chance of success. An experienced hunter, angler, or woodsman increases the chance.

    POINTCRAWL TRAVEL

    POINT CRAWLING

    Points of Interest (POIs) are marked as locations on the map with a number. Point of interest can range from settlements, ruins, natural landmarks, or anything that has content for players to interact with. Lines of travel between POIs indicate routes or paths. The time and length of a path is different, depending on distance, terrain, and other factors.

    ROUTES

    Routes between two points are designated by their two labels (e.g. 01/04 is the route between points 01 and 04.) Typically they are the same both directions, but in cases where one direction might be different (such as uphill, upriver, etc.) label it 01 to 04 to indicate direction.

    • › Routes will indicate travel time, encounters, hazards, and any other particulars.
    • › Encounters can include creatures, NPCs, shifts in weather or terrain, exhaustion, requiring resource expenditure, discovery, and even finding treasure.
    • › The time a route takes to travel is a combination of both distance and difficulty or quality of terrain.
    • › A route that appears shorter than another might take longer to traverse because of difficult terrain or other factors.

    MOVEMENT ACTIONS

    • › Travel: Move through an available route from your current point to another connected point. Refer to the route entry for details on travel time and hazards. Roll on the encounter table for that route.
    • › Camp: Make camp and rest for 8 hours.
    • › Forage: Hunt, fish, or forage for food. Every attempt takes 1 hour. There is a base line 3-in-6 chance of success. An experienced hunter, angler, or woodsman increases the chance.

    SURVIVAL

    • › Players must spend roughly 8 hours every 24 hours resting or become deprived.
    • › Players will need to eat 1 ration (or equivalent) every 24 hours or become deprived.

    STARSHIPS

    STARSHIP SCORES

    • › Shields (SHI) Shields of energy protect the hull of the ship from structural damage. They take 10 mins to recharge after combat.
    • › Hull (HUL) The hull of the ship is the structural integrity and outer physical layer.
    • › Engines (ENG) Engines power the ship by providing it with energy.
    • › Systems (SYS) Systems are the quality of the ship's wiring and technological capability.

    HOLDS

    • › Holds are a ship's inventory capabilities.
    • › Most starship parts occupy 1 hold. Bulky parts occupy 2 hold.
    • › A hold can contain quarters (rooms), modules, or a single unit of trade goods.
    • › A ship's size determines how many holds it has.

    QUARTERS

    Quarters are rooms or areas on the ship. All ships will have a cockpit or bridge by default, along with anything else specified.

    MODULES

    Modules add features or increase Starship Scores. Modules can be weapons, technology, improvements to shields, armoring, hull, engines, and systems. Modules can stack.

    ARMORING

    Before calculating damage to SHI, subtract the target's armoring value from the result of damage rolls. No ship can have more than 3 armor.

    MOVEMENT & MOBILITY

    • › Movement is the amount of hexes a ship may move during their turn in combat. Ships may change their direction using one point of movement.
    • › Mobility is the amount of directions on a hex they may rotate using one unit of movement. One point of mobility allows a ship to rotate for 60 degrees

    CREW REQUIREMENT

    Minimum individuals required to operate the ship, maximum capacity. Ship size lists a range from minimum crew needed for operation, all the way to maximum capacity.

    CREW MATES

    Ships require a crew to operate. Hire a crew mate for 50C a week. They will live on and help operate the ship. If they need to be interacted with individually, they will have Attribute Scores of 10 and 3 HP. If crew mates come on missions, treat them as freelancers.

    BUILDING & UPGRADING

    • › A ship will cost 500C for each Hold. Determine the size of ship you are creating based on Holds and assign a price. (e.g. a Medium Ship will have a base cost of 6,000C.)
    • › Any other modules or quarters will increase the ship's price based on cost of parts.
    • › You cannot buy individual holds. A ship must be upgraded in size which takes 1D6 days and has a labor cost of 100C a day. Upgrading a ship's size also has an additional cost of 2,000C, which is the value of the increase in holds (increments of 4).

    SHIP SIZE

    Starships come in 5 Sizes (Fighter, Small, Medium, Large, Giant). Each size has a fixed number of holds, which will determine capacity for ship upgrades and cargo.

    • › All ships start with Starship Scores of 10. Add modules to upgrade their scores.
    • › All ships come with a cockpit (FighterMedium) or bridge (Large & Giant). Doesn't fill a Hold.
    • › All ships come with an engine room (Fighters just have an engine.) Doesn't fill a Hold.
    • › All ships come freely equipped with an Unstable Shield Gen (1 Hold) and a Flak Cannon (1 Hold).
    • › Some starship parts can only fit certain ship sizes (use your judgement), or have minimum Ship Score requirements.

    STARSHIP COMBAT

    START OF COMBAT

    At the Start of Combat, and each subsequent round, the pilot makes a DEX Save to act first.

    DEPARTMENT PHASES

    On each round, every department will get to perform a department action if at least one player is operating that department. The departments are Pilot, Engineering, Weapons, Systems, and Command. These phase can happen in any order and all occur simultaneously during the player turn.

    FACING

    Unless otherwise noted, attacks from behind go up one dice size, and from head-on they go down one dice size.

    ATTACKING & DAMAGE

    • › Damage is first done to a ship's Shields, then the remaining damage is done to the Hull (see critical damage).
    • › Some weapons might have special properties or specific damage types, and some shields might specifically guard against that type of damage.

    ATTACK MODIFIERS

    • › If fighting from a position of weakness (such as through cover, or with malfunctioning systems or weapons), the attack is impaired.
    • › If fighting from a position of advantage (such as against a malfunctioning ship or through a daring maneuver), the attack is enhanced.
    • › Certain advantageous situations or systems might call for enhanced damage or bonus dice. GM may use at their own discretion as a reward for players' clever thinking.

    SHIP SIZE IN COMBAT

    Fighter Deals impaired damage to medium ships, attacks to larger ships are typically ignored.
    Small Deals impaired damage to large ships, attacks to giant ships are typically ignored.
    Medium Deals enhanced damage to fighters, and impaired damage to giant ships.
    Large Deals critical damage to fighters, deals enhanced damage to small ships.
    Giant Deals critical damage to fighters and small ships, and enhanced damage to medium ships.

    PILOTING ACTION

    The pilot may either move or perform a maneuver. Below is a list of piloting maneuvers.

    • › Spin: Use half of your movement then attempt to turn up to 180 degrees. Make a DEX save, upon failure you don't make the spin.
    • › Evade: Use half of your movement then attempt some tricky moves and rolls. Make a DEX save to impair damage taken that round.
    • › Flee: Double your movement speed and don't make any weapon actions that turn. You may attempt to jump to hyperspace via a Systems Action after this maneuver.

    ENGINEERING ACTION

    The ship's engineer can boost other stations on the ship each turn.

    If the ship has energy reserves, use those first and don't trigger an ENG Save. If no energy reserves are available, this action will reduce the ENG score by 1 and the ship must then make an ENG Save vs critical damage.

    Engines that malfunction from a failed ENG Save can be repaired by a skilled mechanic outside of combat. Repairs take 1D6 hours for each point.

    • › Boost Piloting Station: Pilot may take a second action.
    • › Boost Shields: Restore 1D6 shields. Cannot be used on consecutive turns.
    • › Boost Weapons: One weapon module may make two attacks and use the best result.
    • › Boost Systems: Use two systems in one turn without needing to make a WIL Save.

    WEAPONS ACTION

    A PC operating the ship's weapons may choose a weapon module to operate and make an attack each turn.

    • › Switching Weapons: Switching to a different weapon takes one turn. A character may attempt to change weapons and attack in the same turn by making a DEX Save. Failure results in being knocked down for 1D4 turns.
    • › Multiple Weapons: Multiple PCs can operate different weapons if your ship has more than one. When multiple weapons are being used, only apply the highest result.

    SYSTEMS ACTION

    A system operator may activate a system module on their turn. Consult the particular system module to see the effect. Only one player may operate the ship's systems array. Attempting to use multiple systems in one turn requires a WIL Save from the operator, failure results in no systems being activated that turn and the operator taking fatigue.

    COMMAND ACTION

    Encourage another department for 1 fatigue. This allows one individual to attempt any single roll a second time and keep the result if it's better.

    CRITICAL DAMAGE

    Damage that reduces a starship's Shields below zero decreases a target's Score by the amount remaining based on damage type. The ship must then make a Save against the damage type dealt to avoid taking critical damage. Any starship that fails this save takes critical damage.

    • › If a ship takes critical damage to the HUL, it is breached . 1D20 minutes before oxygen is depleted, which will result in death for organic beings.
    • › If a ship takes critical damage to ENG, it loses power cannot do anything but drift helplessly and shield's won't recharge.
    • › If a ship takes critical damage to SYS, all systems malfunction (including weapons and hyperdrive.)

    If a starship continues to take damage and a Score is reduced to zero, see 'Starship Score Loss' below.

    Some modules may include special abilities or effects that are triggered when a target fails the critical damage save.

    STARSHIP SCORE LOSS

    • › If a ship's HUL is reduced to 0, it is destroyed . Vac-suits and escape pods on the ship may prevent anyone onboard from dying in space.
    • › If a ship's ENG is reduced to 0, it is junked . Engines are totally destroyed. The ship cannont fly and will require replacement.
    • › If a ship's SYS is reduced to 0, it is fried . All modules (including the hyperdrive) are inoperable. The ship's entire systems must be replaced.

    REPAIRING DAMAGE

    PCs may attempt to repair damage to Starship Scores. During combat, one attempt per Score may be made each round. If PCs have the appropriate tools (welding torch, engineering tools, electrical kit, etc.) and skill/training, they will repair 1D4 damage each round and deplete the tool. If a PC only has tools or skill, but not both, they must pass a WIL Save to figure it out. If multiple players attempt the same repair, only the highest result applies. Fully repairing damaged Scores will remove the effects critical damage.

    SPACE TRAVEL

    LOCAL TRAVEL

    Starships traveling within a star system rely on ship thrusters. Travel between planets in a system takes about 1D6 hours on average.

    LIGHTSPEED TRAVEL

    Interstellar travel is accomplished via hyperdrive technology that allows starships to travel at faster-than-light speeds.

    Complex calculations are made based on galactic mapping that can take anywhere from a few minutes to a few hours depending on the route and distance. Travel time is typically 1 day per hex. Adjust this if you want to suit your setting and its technology.

    HYPERLANES

    Routed lanes for hypderdrive travel. Hyperlanes are known coordinates a ship's navigation computer uses to plot routes. Ships can only travel to systems it has routes for. Space is big and you must have the coordinates to locate destinations.

    HYPERDRIVES

    Hyperdrives are onboard systems that allow for starships to make lightspeed jumps.

    Hyperdrive travel still requires coordinates to either be calculated based on ship mapping of known systems, or manually entered by a trained navigator.

    Hyperdrives are engineered as to not make jumps if calculations detect interference such as planetary bodies or other ships in the way.

    FUEL

    A tank of fuel is considered a trade good and occupies 1 hold. Each tank of fuel has 10 uses. Each lightspeed jump via a hyperlane takes 1 use of fuel. This is to account for the complex calculations involved in hyperspace travel. If players want to travel off lanes via hexes, each hex takes 1 use of fuel.

    DOCKS & ORBITAL STATIONS

    Most planets and moons will have dedicated docks for starships to land. Those that don't (due to limited space, environmental reasons, political reasons, etc.) will have orbital stations. These stations serve as hubs for starships to dock, refuel, and repair.

    PSIONICS

    ABOUT PSIONICS

    Psionic powers come from within. The awakening of an individual's ultimate potential. These powers are typically the result of evolution or genetic mutation, but can also come from other sources, such as stress, trauma, or powerful insight.

    • › Psionic powers must be channeled with one hand free. For each use, take 1 fatigue.

    CORRUPTION

    GAINING CORRUPTION

    Some powers are beyond our ability to control. Corruption is the result of tampering with forces beyond your comprehension and those forces changing you. When dangerous powers are manipulated or strange cosmic encounters occur, there is a risk of those forces corrupting you, hollowing you out and stealing away your humanity.

    Attempts to manipulate such forces require a WIL Save vs Corruption. Upon failure, roll for hollowing and then mark a point of corruption. Corruption is permanent.

    HOLLOWING

    Roll a D20 + current corruption on the Hollowing table to see how you are altered.

    Example: You've failed two saves vs. corruption in the past, so your current corruption is 2. You again fail a save and roll a D20 for hollowing. Your result is a 7 and you add +2 for the result #9. Finally, add another point to your corruption total, which is now 3.

    THE THREE ASPECTS

    There are three primal forces that work in tandem to create universal balance. Tapping into these powers risks upsetting that balance as the wielder allows these forces to use their body as a catalyst. Which forces you allow in aren't up to you. Sometimes the void of space simply hollows you out a little more each time you call to it. Other times, one particular aspect crawls its way into you to claim a small part of your humanity for its own inscrutable patterns.

    Sacred Geometry

    The binding force that holds the universe together is imposing, immovable, and uncompromising. Some call it order, others harmonica universalis, the music of the spheres, perfection, and life.

    Entropy

    The other face of order is utter oblivion. This devouring chaos manifests as black holes, destruction, and the inevitable unraveling to come.

    Non-Euclidean

    Beyond the paradigm of order and chaos lies another force, completely unknowable and terrifying. Is it the inscrutable arithmetic of alternate dimensions, or simply another mystery that will consume everything?

    GENERAL PRINCIPLES

    Ability Scores: 3 is deficient, 6 is weak, 10 is average, 14 is noteworthy, 18 is legendary. Any ability score not listed is treated as 10.

    Hit Protection: Give average creatures 3 HP, hardy ones 6 HP, and serious threats get 10+ HP. Remember that HP is Hit Protection, not Hit Points. It's a measure of resilience, luck, and gumption, not health.

    Other: Use flavor and style to help them stand out. Use critical damage to lean into the threat or strangeness of any aggressive NPC.

    ADDITIONAL ADVICE

    • › Is it good at avoiding a hit? Give it HP .
    • › Does it soak up damage? Give it Armor .
    • › Is it strong? Give it a high STR .
    • › Is it nimble? Give it high DEX .
    • › Is it charismatic or mentally powerful? Give it high WIL .

    Damage dice are roughly the same, though armed attacks do at least 1d6 damage.

    CONVERTING FROM CLASSIC OSR GAMES

    • › Give 3 HP per HD for most creatures.
    • › Most humanoids have at least 4HP.
    • › WIL can be used as a baseline for morale.

    CONVERTING FROM MODERN SCI-FI GAMES

    • › Any sort of panic or stress condition can be handled by making a WIL save vs. corruption, fatigue, or deprivation.
    • › Generally the best way to convert any NPC is to describe them in fictional terms (strong, slow, not armored, carries a large gun) and then turn that description into Monolith's mechanics.

    FACTION RULES

    TYPES OF FACTIONS

    Factions generally fall into one of the below categories, but there can be more, and often they're blended:

    • › Governments
    • › Corporations
    • › Criminal (gangs, pirates, syndicates, mafias, cartels)
    • › Mercenary
    • › Media & Entertainment (news, social media, etc.)
    • › Manufacturing (engineering, weapons, starships, A.I., drugs)
    • › Services (transport, information, etc)
    • › Cults
    • › Cultural Organizations (unions, clubs)
    • › Religions

    RESOURCES

    Faction resources can range from wealth, manpower, technology, influence, reputation, or other abstract sources of power.

    GOALS

    • › Factions are always working towards some kind of goal.
    • › The goal should be something the players can get involved with, and those goals will typically butt up against the goals of opposing factions.
    • › Individuals inside a faction have their own personal agendas.

    GOAL PROGRESSION

    • › Factions may have several goals, but can only be actively working toward one goal at a time.
    • › Goals are indicated with boxes ranging from 2 to 10, depending on complexity.
    • › Between sessions or at certain intervals of time based on the goal (weeks, months) roll 2d6 and consult the chart below. Add +1 for relevant resources or help from players. Subtract -1 for interference from rival factions or the players.
    • › When a faction completes a goal, it will usually mean adding a new resource. Goals can include reducing other factions resources, power, or influence.
    2 3-6 7-11 12
    Setback (-1 box) No progress Progress (+1 box) Windfall (+2 box)

    FACTION QUALITIES

    Some factions will be violent, diplomatic, crafty, ambitious, underhanded, benevolent, peaceful, hidden, famous, infamous, etc. Give each faction a few descriptors to paint a picture, the rest can be developed through play.

    FACTION SIZE

    Faction size determines their range of power and influence. Feuding with a size above or below usually results in some sort of bonus or penalty. Faction size can fluctuate with major gains or losses.

    • › Size 1: Local to planet
    • › Size 2: Local on multiple planets and surrounding orbits
    • › Size 3: Large Influence over entire star systems
    • › Size 4: Major influence over several systems or whole sub-sectors
    • › Size 5: Grand influence extends from multiple sub-sectors to several entire sectors.
